1. The Challenge
India’s retail investor base has grown at an unprecedented pace:
total Demat
accounts have crossed 16+ crore, with more than 70% of incremental retail ac-
counts opening from non-metro, Tier-2, and Tier-3 cities.
Access to markets has
outrun access to financial confidence.
For most first-time and retail investors, financial decisions are shaped by mis-
information, fraudulent schemes, social-media “tips,” emotional decision-making,
complex terminology, or simply the absence of trustworthy guidance.
SEBI’s own
studies show that 9 out of 10 individual traders in equity F&O incur net losses
:
a signal that market access alone is not building resilience.
The challenge, therefore, is not to help people invest :
it is to help them be-
come resilient investors:
people who can identify risk, avoid fraud, understand
financial products, build healthy habits, and decide independently and safely.
The objective is to help users lose less, decide rationally, understand
what they are getting into, recognise risk before it costs them, and build
habits that hold up under pressure.
2. Mission
Build a technology-driven product that strengthens the financial resilience of
Indian investors, with a particular focus on users from Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities
and emerging digital-finance users.
The product should address a real problem
an investor may face before, during, or after a financial decision.
3. Core Design Principle
A strong solution should answer at least one of the following.
The strongest
solutions connect several of these into a single, coherent product.
• Help an investor detect a financial fraud or scam before becoming a victim
• Help an investor recognise misleading or manipulative financial information
• Explain a financial product or regulatory process in simple, plain language
• Build better financial habits and more deliberate decision-making
• Reduce panic, greed, herd behaviour, or impulsive financial decisions
• Clarify an investor’s rights, responsibilities, and protection mechanisms
• Help an investor recover intelligently and safely after something goes wrong
• Build financial confidence without creating dependency on “tips” or influ-
encers

5. Challenge Tracks
Teams may pick one track, combine tracks, or enter the Open Track.
Depth on one
real user journey is valued over breadth across many features.
Track A : Digital Fraud & Scam Resilience
Focus:
Detecting, warning against, and intercepting deceptive financial vectors
before money changes hands.
Problem: Scammers exploit fake SEBI registration numbers, cloned broker portals,
Telegram/WhatsApp pump-and-dump groups, deepfake “finfluencers,” and remote-
access apps.
Victims typically realise it only when withdrawals are blocked.
Suggested Directions:
• Scam & Claim Verifier :
Cross-check a social handle, message, link, or claim
against known red-flag patterns and official registries in real time.
• Tip-Group Risk Profiler :
Let a user forward a screenshot or message from a
WhatsApp/Telegram “tip” channel and return an explainable risk score.
Track B : Investor Awareness, Rights & Grievance Redressal
Focus:
Making investor rights, protections, and complaint mechanisms usable by
a first-time user.
Problem: Investor-protection processes such as SCORES, nominee registration, and
IEPF unclaimed-dividend recovery are often too complex for someone using them
for the first time :
especially senior citizens, homemakers, and legal heirs in
smaller towns.
Suggested Directions:
• Grievance Assistant :
Guide a user through framing a clear, well-documented
complaint against a broker or depository participant.
• Nominee & Family Wealth Tracker :
Help first-generation investors record
nominees across Demat, bank, and mutual-fund folios to prevent unclaimed in-

Track C : Investor Education for Bharat
Focus:
Replacing jargon-heavy disclosures and static PDFs with understanding-
first, regional-language learning.
Problem: Financial terminology :
NAV, risk, diversification, volatility, com-
pounding, fees, nomination :
is written for the financially literate, pushing
first-time users toward simplified but often fake advice instead.
Suggested Directions:
• Voice-First Explainer :
A conversational guide in regional languages that
explains market and product concepts through everyday analogies, built for
low-bandwidth use.
• Consequence Simulator :
Let a user safely experience what volatility, lever-
age, or a market crash feels like, with zero real money at risk.
Track D : Financial Habits & Behavioural Resilience
Focus:
Helping investors pause, reflect, and build discipline instead of acting
on impulse.
Problem: Fear, greed, FOMO, herd behaviour, and revenge trading push investors :
including those funding trades with emergency savings or instant loans :
toward
decisions they would not make with a clear head.
Suggested Directions:
• Cooling-Off Circuit Breaker :
Detect erratic patterns (rapid loss-chasing
trades, late-night impulsive activity) and introduce a deliberate pause before
the next action.
• Decision Journal :
Prompt a user to state their reasoning and time horizon
before committing funds, building a habit of patient, considered decisions.
Track E : Misinformation & Financial Content Literacy
Focus:
Helping users evaluate the flood of financial content on WhatsApp,
YouTube, Instagram, and Telegram.
Problem: Investors struggle to tell education apart from promotion, and
confident-sounding claims apart from evidence-backed ones :
often receiving
no better answer than a simplistic “true/false.”
Suggested Directions:
• Claim Evidence-Checker :
Assess whether a financial claim is backed by ver-
ifiable evidence and communicate the uncertainty honestly, not as a binary
verdict.
• Promotion vs Education Classifier :
Help a user distinguish content that is
educating them from content that is selling them something.

6. Target User
Solve for someone for whom existing financial systems and information are not
easy to navigate :
a first-time investor from a Tier-2/3 city, a person more
comfortable in a regional language than English, a senior citizen vulnerable to
impersonation, or someone who has recently faced or nearly faced a scam.
Teams
should clearly identify who they are solving for and why that user’s problem
matters.
7. Important Constraints
This hackathon is about investor resilience, not investment returns.
A product
may use financial or market data for educational, awareness, simulation, safety,
or analytical purposes :
but it must never turn that data into a personalised
investment recommendation.
Allowed
Not Allowed
“Here is what volatility means and
how it can affect an investor.”
“Based on your profile, buy XYZ
stock.”
