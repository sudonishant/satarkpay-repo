Emergency mode

Money has already left.

Everything below is ordered by what actually changes the outcome, not by what is easy to write down. The first hour matters more than the next ten, and nothing here requires you to be calm.

Start with the clock, not the paperwork. If the transfer happened in the last few hours, calling 1930 now is worth more than anything else on this page. Write nothing down first — call.

The order to act in

Call 1930 — the national cyber-fraud helplineReport the transaction and ask for a hold. Have the transaction reference and the amount ready, but do not delay the call to prepare them. This is the step with a time limit.
File on cybercrime.gov.inChoose the financial-fraud category. Keep your statement and the original message ready to attach. The reference number you get here matters later.
Tell your bank in writingUse the fraud number printed on your card or on the bank's own website — never a number from a chat. Ask for a complaint reference and note it down. Under RBI's rules a customer must report an unauthorised transaction promptly; the sooner it is reported, the better the position.
Freeze what is still reachableBlock the card, change the UPI PIN and net-banking password — from a different device if you installed anything a caller asked you to install. Revoke unknown AutoPay mandates while you are in there.
Preserve the evidence before it disappearsDo not delete the message or the chat. Screenshot the transaction in your app, copy the UTR, note the caller's number and the exact time. Scammers delete their side immediately; yours is the only copy left.
Tell one other personNot because they can fix it, but because the second half of these scams — the "recovery agent" — targets people who handled it alone and quietly.
Report the sender to 1909Forward the SMS or report the number so it is blocked for everyone, not just for you.

What to have in your hand

From your bank app

  • The UTR / transaction reference of the debit
  • Amount and the exact date and time
  • Payee name and the UPI ID as shown
  • A screenshot of the transaction detail

From your phone

  • The original message, unedited
  • The caller's number, and any chat thread
  • The link, if there was one — leave it intact
  • The name of any app you were asked to install

Written down

  • Time of the call and of the transfer
  • Who you spoke to and what they claimed to be
  • Which bank or app you have already called
  • Every reference number you have been given

Two things that will happen next

Somebody will offer to recover your money. They will find you within days, sometimes hours. They will know the amount. They may have a letterhead. They will ask for an advance fee, a "court charge" or your details again. Every reported case of this ends with a second, larger loss. No agency charges a fee to recover money, and nothing about your case moves faster because you paid someone.
The money may not come back, and that is not something this app can change. A hold depends on where the funds moved and how quickly it was reported. Banks and payment intermediaries are notified when you report early, but a refund is never guaranteed — that is the honest position, and it is why the sixty seconds before the PIN are worth more than any recovery process after it.

If it was not money at all

Some of these are worth reporting even when nothing was lost — a blocked number is a number that cannot be used on the next person.

Identity documents exposed

Aadhaar or PAN shared over chat: lock the Aadhaar biometrics online, and check which mobile connections are issued in your name on Sanchar Saathi.

Remote-access app installed

Uninstall it, then change passwords from a different device, then remove the device from any signed-in sessions in your bank and email accounts.
